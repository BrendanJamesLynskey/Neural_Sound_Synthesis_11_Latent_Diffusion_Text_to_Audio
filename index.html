Latent Diffusion: Text-to-Audio

Diffusion made stunning images by denoising in a compressed latent, not on raw pixels. Audio borrowed the trick wholesale: run the reverse process inside a learned, tiny latent from a VAE, steer it with a shared text–audio embedding, and a plain prompt becomes a barking dog, a rainstorm, or a full stereo track. Three live demos build the picture from the spectrogram up.

1The idea — move the diffusion off the waveform

Part 8 ran diffusion directly on audio: start from noise, denoise toward a waveform or a full-resolution spectrogram. It works, but it is punishingly expensive — every one of the dozens-to-thousands of reverse steps evaluates a network over a signal with tens of thousands of dimensions. Latent diffusion is the fix that unlocked text-to-image (Rombach et al., 2022) and then text-to-audio: don't diffuse the signal, diffuse a compressed code of it.

The recipe is two-stage. First, a variational autoencoder (Part 9) learns to squeeze audio into a small continuous latent $\mathbf{z}$ and reconstruct it faithfully — a perceptual compressor that throws away what the ear won't miss. Then a diffusion model is trained to generate in that latent, and the VAE decoder turns each generated $\mathbf{z}$ back into sound. The generative network never touches a raw sample; it works in a space that may be dozens of times smaller.

$$\mathbf{z} = \mathcal{E}(\mathbf{x}),\qquad \mathcal{L}_{\text{LDM}} = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{z}_0,\,t,\,\epsilon}\big\|\,\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{z}_t,\,t,\,\mathbf{c})\,\big\|^2,\qquad \hat{\mathbf{x}} = \mathcal{D}(\hat{\mathbf{z}})$$

This is the ordinary denoising objective from Part 8 — predict the noise $\epsilon$ added at level $t$ — with two changes: it lives on the latent $\mathbf{z}_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,\mathbf{z}_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon$ instead of the signal, and it takes a conditioning $\mathbf{c}$ — usually a text embedding — injected through cross-attention. Cut the dimensionality and you cut both the memory and the number of steps needed for a given quality; add the conditioning and the same machine becomes a prompt-driven instrument.

Why it is cheap. A U-Net or DiT denoiser costs roughly the square of the token count in its attention. Compress time–frequency by, say, 8× along each axis before diffusing and you shrink that quadratic cost by orders of magnitude — the whole reason latent diffusion runs on a single GPU.
The catch, inherited from Part 9. Quality is now capped by the autoencoder. If the VAE can't represent a transient or a sibilant, no amount of diffusion in its latent will recover it — the decoder can only ever hand back sounds it was trained to make.

The rest of this part is the anatomy of that machine, one component per section: the image the diffusion sees (§2), the latent it actually lives in (§3), and the text embedding that steers it (§4).

2Spectrograms as images — the Riffusion trick

The fastest route to text-to-audio, historically, skipped the custom latent entirely. Riffusion (Forsgren & Martiros, 2022) fine-tuned Stable Diffusion itself — an image model — on pictures of mel spectrograms paired with text. A spectrogram is, after all, just a greyscale image: time on one axis, frequency on the other, brightness for energy. Diffuse the image, then invert it back to sound.

Inversion is the only audio-specific step. The magnitude spectrogram has thrown away phase, so a vocoder must invent it: Riffusion used Griffin–Lim, which iterates STFT ↔ inverse-STFT to hallucinate a phase consistent with the given magnitudes. Because Stable Diffusion's latent is spatially smooth, two prompts can be interpolated and the audio morphs seamlessly — and a spectrogram can be generated to loop, giving endless variations of a groove.

The demo below is a teaching model of the reverse process operating in the image domain. On the left is the target spectrogram of a short clip we generated. On the right, we corrupt it to pure noise and then run reverse-diffusion steps that progressively denoise it back. To make a clean, convergent trajectory you can watch, the demo uses the known target as an oracle — a real trained $\epsilon_\theta$ estimates the same denoising direction, imperfectly. Set the step count, press Denoise, then Invert & play to hear the current spectrogram reconstructed with oracle phase.

Try this: denoise with just 4 steps, invert and listen — the reconstruction is grainy, the harmonics smeared. Now push the slider to 60 steps and repeat: each reverse step removes a slice of noise variance, the harmonic combs sharpen, and the clip rings clean. That step–quality trade is the single dial every diffusion sampler exposes.

the reverse step, and why more steps means cleaner audio

Forward diffusion adds Gaussian noise on a schedule $\bar\alpha_t$, so a level-$t$ latent is $\mathbf{z}_t=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,\mathbf{z}_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon$. The trained network predicts $\epsilon$, which gives an estimate of the clean signal $\hat{\mathbf{z}}_0=(\mathbf{z}_t-\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon_\theta)/\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}$, and each reverse step nudges $\mathbf{z}_t$ toward $\mathbf{z}_{t-1}$ using it:

$$\mathbf{z}_{t-1}=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t-1}}\,\hat{\mathbf{z}}_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_{t-1}}\,\epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{z}_t,t,\mathbf{c}).$$

Each step is a linear approximation to a curved probability-flow trajectory. Few steps means large jumps that overshoot the curve — audibly, smeared transients and rough noise floors. Many steps hug the curve and land closer to the true data manifold. Fast samplers (DDIM, DPM-Solver) buy back most of the quality at low step counts by using higher-order updates; the demo's linear oracle blend is the intuition, not the production sampler.

3The VAE latent — a small, smooth space to walk in

Riffusion diffuses full spectrogram images; modern audio systems diffuse a purpose-built continuous latent from an audio VAE (Part 9). Two properties make that latent the right place to work. It is compact — Stable Audio's autoencoder compresses 44.1 kHz stereo by a large factor, so the diffusion model sees a short sequence of fat vectors instead of a huge spectrogram. And it is smooth: the VAE's KL prior packs the codes into a well-behaved region where nearby points decode to perceptually nearby sounds.

Smoothness is what makes generation and interpolation work. If a straight line between two latents passed through "holes" that decode to garbage, diffusion — which spends its whole trajectory moving through that space — would keep falling into them. Instead you can take two codes and walk between them, and the sound morphs continuously. Drag the slider to walk the latent path below; the spectrogram at the current point is decoded live, and you can hear either endpoint.

t = 0.00 — endpoint A. Drag the slider to interpolate the latent; the spectrogram is decoded at each point.

Endpoint A is a low, dark, near-harmonic tone; B is a high, bright, slightly inharmonic and noisier one. Notice the morph is monotone — brightness, pitch and noisiness slide together with no sudden jumps. That is the geometric property a diffusion model relies on, and the reason latent interpolation is a headline feature of these systems rather than an accident.

Latent vs pixel diffusion, in one line. The VAE decoder shoulders fidelity; the diffusion model only has to place a distribution over a friendly low-dimensional code. The same division of labour recurs across the field — codecs+LMs (Part 10), latent+diffusion here.

4Text conditioning — CLAP and classifier-free guidance

A latent diffusion model generates something from noise; text conditioning decides what. The bridge is a shared text–audio embedding space. CLAP (Contrastive Language–Audio Pretraining) trains an audio encoder and a text encoder together so that a clip and its caption land at nearly the same point — the audio twin of CLIP. AudioLDM (Liu et al., 2023) leaned on this so heavily it called the CLAP embedding the "language of audio": at training time it conditions on the audio's CLAP embedding, and at inference swaps in the text's — the shared space makes them interchangeable.

The contrastive objective is the InfoNCE loss: pull matched text/audio pairs together, push mismatched ones apart, across a batch of $N$.

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{CLAP}} = -\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\frac{\exp\!\big(\langle \mathbf{t}_i,\mathbf{a}_i\rangle/\tau\big)}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\exp\!\big(\langle \mathbf{t}_i,\mathbf{a}_j\rangle/\tau\big)}$$

with $\mathbf{t}_i,\mathbf{a}_i$ the L2-normalised text and audio embeddings and $\tau$ a temperature. The map below is a cartoon of the result: four prompts (squares) and their matching audio clips (circles) sit close in a 2-D joint space. Pick a prompt and press Generate — a sample is pulled from the prior mean toward the prompt's region. The strength of that pull is classifier-free guidance.

■ text embedding ● audio embedding ○ generated sample. Pick a prompt and press Generate.

Classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2021) is the workhorse that makes prompts bite. You train one network to predict noise both with the condition and without it (dropping $\mathbf{c}$ some fraction of the time), then at sampling time extrapolate away from the unconditional prediction:

$$\tilde\epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{z}_t,\mathbf{c}) = \epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{z}_t,\varnothing) + w\,\big(\epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{z}_t,\mathbf{c}) - \epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{z}_t,\varnothing)\big)$$

At $w=0$ the sample ignores the prompt (the mean of the prior); at $w=1$ it is the plain conditional; at $w>1$ it is pushed past the conditional, harder onto the prompt. Slide the guidance up in the demo and the generated point marches out toward — and then overshoots — the prompt cluster, exactly as the equation says. Overshoot is the double edge: high $w$ nails the prompt but flattens diversity and can distort, which is why real systems tune $w$ to a sweet spot around $3$–$7$.

The guidance you hear. Press Generate on each prompt — the demo synthesises a crude representative sound for that label. In a real system the same guidance vector isn't a toy 2-D arrow but a high-dimensional cross-attention conditioning that steers every reverse step of the latent diffusion.

5The systems — from spectrogram hack to stereo tracks

In two years the field went from repurposing an image model to purpose-built stereo music generators. The through-line is constant — diffusion in a latent, conditioned on text — and the differences are in the autoencoder, the backbone, and the conditioning.

SystemYearWhat it diffusesConditioning & backbone
Riffusion2022 Mel-spectrogram images in Stable Diffusion's VAE latent Fine-tuned Stable Diffusion + CLIP text; U-Net; Griffin–Lim inversion
AudioLDM2023 A mel-spectrogram VAE latent, trained on audio only Conditioned on CLAP embeddings ("language of audio"); U-Net; HiFi-GAN vocoder
AudioLDM 22023 Same latent, unified across speech / music / SFX A "language of audio" (AudioMAE) target predicted by a GPT front-end; more general conditioning
Make-An-Audio · Tango2023 Spectrogram / mel latents CLAP or an instruction-tuned LLM (FLAN-T5, in Tango) as the text encoder
MusicLDM2023 Music-domain latent CLAP conditioning + beat-synchronous mixup to fight training-data memorisation
Stable Audio2023–24 A highly-compressed waveform autoencoder latent — full 44.1 kHz stereo Text + timing embeddings for variable-length tracks; DiT (transformer) backbone

Stable Audio (Evans et al., 2023/24) is the clearest departure. It abandons the spectrogram entirely for a heavily-compressed autoencoder that maps stereo waveform straight to latent and back, so there is no separate vocoder and no phase to hallucinate. It replaces the U-Net with a diffusion transformer (DiT), and — crucially — adds a timing-conditioning embedding that tells the model the desired duration and offset, letting one model produce variable-length clips up to minutes long instead of a fixed window. That combination — waveform latent, DiT, timing control — is what turned text-to-audio from short sound-effects into full musical structures.

Every one of these is the same sentence with different nouns: encode audio to a latent, learn to denoise that latent, steer the denoising with a text embedding, decode back to sound. The research is in choosing each noun well.

6Diffusion vs the token-LM route

Part 10 generated audio the other way: quantise it into discrete tokens with a codec, then predict the next token with a transformer language model. Latent diffusion and token-LMs are the two dominant paradigms for text-to-audio, and they make opposite bets on almost every axis.

Diffusion (this part)

  • Continuous latent; non-autoregressive — the whole clip is refined in parallel over reverse steps.
  • Strong on texture and timbre: dense, stationary content like pads, rain, or rich instrumental mixes.
  • Quality–speed dial is the step count; fast samplers shrink it.
  • Global coherence comes for free (all positions denoised together); very long-range musical form is harder.

Token language model (Part 10)

  • Discrete tokens; autoregressive — one token at a time, left to right.
  • Strong on long structure: verse–chorus form, lyrics, sequences that must stay consistent over time.
  • Quality–speed dial is sequence length × codebooks; slow to sample.
  • Inherits the transformer's toolbox — in-context prompting, scaling laws — directly.

The lines are blurring. MusicGen (Part 10) is an AR transformer over codec tokens; Stable Audio is a diffusion transformer over a continuous latent — both use a transformer backbone, differing only in whether it predicts discrete tokens autoregressively or denoises a continuous latent in parallel. Hybrids exist too: diffusion over continuous codec features, or LM-planned coarse structure refined by a diffusion decoder. The primitives from Part 1 are being recombined, not chosen between.

Rule of thumb. If the task is a texture, a stem, or a short richly-timbred clip, reach for diffusion. If it needs to hold a form or follow lyrics over tens of seconds, an autoregressive LM still has the edge — and increasingly the answer is one transformer wearing whichever hat the task needs.
